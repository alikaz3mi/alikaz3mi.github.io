## About Me

I am Ali Kazemi, a researcher working at the intersection of biomedical engineering, computational neuroscience, and data science. My background sits at the meeting point of two things that rarely travel together: a research training in biomedical signal processing and computational neuroscience (M.Sc., GPA 18.04/20), and five years building production machine learning systems where every method had to be measured, validated, and made to work on real data at scale.

I am looking for a funded PhD across that range — biomedical AI, neural-signal and medical-image analysis, or applied machine learning. I am as drawn to a comp-neuro or medical-imaging group as to an AI lab, because my best work has been where the two meet.

Engineering taught me to run controlled experiments and to build the infrastructure that lets an idea be tested rather than just described, and it left me unwilling to make a claim I can't back with a number. I want to apply that in academic work and publish what comes out of it.

### Research interests

- **Computational neuroscience and neural-signal analysis** — deep learning for EEG and electrophysiology: spike sorting, neural decoding, brain–computer interfaces. My [Mind2Text](https://github.com/alikaz3mi/mind2text) project applies LLMs with symbolic tokenization to EEG classification, and I want to push this line further.
- **Medical imaging and biomedical data science** — diffusion-tensor and deep-learning methods for medical images and clinical signals, building on my M.Sc. work modeling neonatal brain development.
- **Machine learning and LLMs** — efficient inference and fine-tuning, retrieval-augmented and agentic systems, reliability and reduction of hallucination.
- **Trustworthy and resource-efficient AI** — methods that hold up under cost, latency, and data constraints, which is where most of my applied work has lived and what clinical and edge deployment demand.

### What I bring

- A research record: an M.Sc. thesis on **diffusion-tensor modeling of perisylvian language-pathway development in term neonates**, a manuscript in preparation, and an independent research project (Mind2Text) on EEG decoding with LLMs.
- The ability to **build the experimental infrastructure others publish on** — training pipelines, evaluation harnesses, and serving systems. In industry I architected RAG and LLM pipelines, cut per-user inference cost by 96% ($2.50 → $0.10), reduced an OCR word-error rate from 20% to 3%, and fine-tuned recognition models on 49M+ images to 99.97% accuracy.
- Working depth in PyTorch/TensorFlow, transformers and LoRA, and the full MLOps stack (Docker, Kubernetes, model serving), plus a habit of test-driven, reproducible code.
- **TOEFL iBT: 105/120.**
